The control problem

Intelligence can propose.
Ownership must still decide.

The age of capable autonomous systems — toward AGI and after — still needs governance of effects. Prompt rules are not an enforcement boundary. A general policy engine can say permit or forbid and then trust the caller to obey.

Decision OS treats action as use of a delegated right: legitimacy (ownership or consent) may only deny, the grant is bound to one payload and spent once, and the audit is not the agent’s to edit. Ethics stay in injected policy. The runtime makes that policy hard to skip. It does not claim to save the world or solve AGI safety.

Core mechanics

⊥

Deny-dominant composition

Independent evaluators meet on a canonical verdict lattice. A plugin cannot grant authority or weaken another denial.

⌁

Action binding

The signature covers canonical action content. A valid decision for one payload cannot authorize another.

1×

Atomic spend

Each execution token is consumed exactly once through a durable SpentStore boundary.

⛓

Execution truth

The audit chain distinguishes allowed-but-refused from allowed-and-executed and records the payload digest.

Known limitations

What this does not claim.

  • Not a prompt firewall. It governs configured effects, not model thoughts or arbitrary external access.
  • Not a universal sandbox. Docker evidence is Linux-profile-specific and does not prove resistance to kernel escape.
  • Not a distributed control plane. Multi-machine replay storage, consensus, TLS, and key rotation require deployment infrastructure.
  • Not policy truth. The runtime enforces supplied legitimacy and authority rules; it does not prove their moral correctness.
  • Not AGI safety, and not Cedar. It mediates configured effects so injected ownership/consent is hard to skip. It does not govern minds, and it is not a general IAM language.
